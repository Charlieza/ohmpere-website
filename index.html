One charger per stop isn't a network.

We drive EVs. On the road and around Cape Town we kept finding the same thing: one charger, already in use, or out of order.

Electric cars are no longer rare in the Western Cape, but public charging hasn't kept pace with where people actually go. Many sites have a single unit. A lot of the public bays in the northern suburbs are slower AC chargers. Some are listed online and don't work when you arrive.

When one car is plugged in, the next driver waits. When the charger is down, everyone waits. Adding capacity at busy places matters as much as adding new places.

Six bays to start. Room for twelve.

An Ohmpere hub mixes slower AC bays for people who stay a while with DC fast chargers for people who don't. The layout is fixed only after we've measured the site's electrical supply.

  • AC baysFour 22 kW bays for shoppers, gym members, diners and staff who stay an hour or more.
  • DC fastTwo 60 to 120 kW chargers for drivers who need range quickly. Sized to what the site's supply can carry.
  • Load controlSmart load management shares power with the building and throttles charging before the centre reaches its limit, so we avoid expensive grid upgrades where possible.
  • PaymentBank card, QR code, app or RFID card. No subscription and no membership needed.
  • UptimeEvery charger is monitored remotely. Faults raise an alert and a technician call-out. Our target is at least 97% availability per site.
  • ConnectorsType 2 on AC and CCS2 on DC, the plugs used by BYD, Geely and most new EVs sold in South Africa.
  • GrowthDucting and board space for up to twelve bays, a solar canopy and a battery, added when the numbers support them.

Questions we get asked.

Something missing? Ask us directly.

For drivers

Will my car work with your chargers?

Yes, if it uses the standard plugs: Type 2 for AC and CCS2 for DC. BYD, Geely and most new EVs sold in South Africa use both.

Do I need to download an app?

No. You can pay by tapping a bank card or scanning the QR code on the charger. The app is there if you want charging history, alerts and receipts in one place.

How much will it cost?

Each hub shows its price per kWh on the charger and in the app before you start. You only pay for the energy your car takes.

What if a charger isn't working?

Every charger reports its status to us in real time and a fault triggers a call-out. You can also report a problem from the QR page on the charger.

When does the first hub open?

Our first sites are in development in Cape Town's northern suburbs. Leave your email and we'll tell you the day it goes live.

For property owners

Do we pay anything upfront?

No. Ohmpere funds the chargers and installation. You provide the bays and access to the electrical supply under a site agreement.

What if our supply is already close to its limit?

Smart load management lets the chargers use spare capacity and back off when the building needs it. Where that isn't enough, we size the DC chargers down or plan a battery. We measure first, then design.

How long does it take?

Typically six to twelve months from the first meeting to switch-on. Most of that is the electrical study, approvals and equipment lead times, and several steps run in parallel.

Who maintains the chargers?

We do, for the full term. Remote monitoring, scheduled maintenance, repairs and customer support are all on us.

Can we add solar later?

Yes. We plan every hub with space and ducting for a solar canopy and a battery, so they can be added without digging up the parking again.
